# Task-Flow Benchmark Method

This document compares interaction structure, not human performance. No completion-time or success-rate claim is made until the protocol is run with consenting participants.

## Structural benchmark

| Task | Conventional portal burden to inspect | Sarathi design response | What to measure in a pilot |
|---|---|---|---|
| Find the next urgent task | Citizen interprets records and dates across services | One prioritised deadline with reason | Time to identify next action |
| Renew a driving licence | Choose service/state, interpret requirements, prepare evidence | Plain-language checklist with official handoff | Wrong turns and missing documents |
| Track an application | Locate or remember an acknowledgement reference | Saved synthetic applications are shown first; manual reference remains available | Reference-recovery rate |
| Prepare an IDP | Discover Form 4A and map repeated profile/trip fields | Citizen-controlled local draft and review gate | Fields corrected before handoff |
| Understand a challan | Interpret notice fields and payment destination | Structured summary, confidence cue and official verification | Comprehension and duplicate-payment concern |

## Controlled study design

Use the same five tasks, device, starting state and neutral moderator script for both experiences. Counterbalance order to reduce learning effects. Record task completion, time, wrong turns, requests for help, trust calibration and whether participants can explain the non-official boundary. Report median and range, not only averages.

## Success thresholds for a pilot

- At least 80% identify the urgent task without help.
- At least 80% reach the correct official handoff.
- At least 90% understand that Sarathi has not submitted or paid on their behalf.
- Zero participants enter real OTPs, passwords or payment credentials into the prototype.
- Every observed critical misunderstanding produces a design change or documented rationale.
